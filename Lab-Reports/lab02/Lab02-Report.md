# Lab02 – Identifying and Preventing Phishing Attacks

## Objective
To understand phishing attacks, recognize common phishing indicators, and explore methods of preventing phishing. A simple local login page was created for educational demonstration.

## Practical Work

### 1. Created a Local Login Demonstration
Created a simple HTML login form named `login.html` containing username and password fields.

The page was created for training purposes only and does not connect to a real website or service.

### 2. Viewed the Demonstration Page
Opened the local HTML file in a web browser and verified that the form displayed username and password fields.

No real credentials were entered or collected.

### 3. Phishing Awareness
Phishing is a type of social engineering attack in which an attacker attempts to trick users into revealing sensitive information or performing unsafe actions.

Common phishing indicators include:
- Suspicious or misspelled URLs.
- Unexpected emails or messages.
- Urgent requests for personal information.
- Unexpected attachments or links.
- Poor grammar or unusual formatting.
- Websites that imitate legitimate services.
- Requests for passwords or OTPs through suspicious channels.

### 4. Prevention Methods
- Verify website URLs before entering information.
- Avoid clicking unexpected links.
- Never share passwords or OTPs.
- Enable multi-factor authentication.
- Use a password manager.
- Keep browsers and operating systems updated.
- Report suspicious emails and messages.
- Verify unusual requests through a trusted communication channel.

### 5. Demonstration Limitations
A local login form was used to demonstrate the appearance and structure of a login page. The Social Engineering Toolkit (SET) site-cloning and credential-capture workflow was not performed. No credentials were harvested or transmitted.

## Analysis
A login form alone does not prove that a website is legitimate. Phishing websites may imitate the visual appearance of trusted websites to deceive users. Users should verify the domain, check the context of the request, and avoid entering sensitive information into suspicious pages.

Modern websites may use HTTPS, multi-factor authentication, anti-phishing protections, security monitoring, and other safeguards. However, HTTPS alone does not prove that a website is trustworthy.

## Conclusion
This exercise provided an introduction to phishing awareness through a local login-page demonstration and analysis of common phishing indicators and prevention methods. Safe browsing habits, verification of URLs, and multi-factor authentication help reduce phishing risks.
